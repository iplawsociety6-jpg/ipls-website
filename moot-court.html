About AIPLA

Founded in 1897, AIPLA is a national bar association constituted primarily of practitioners in private and corporate practice, in government service, and in the academic community. AIPLA represents a wide and diverse spectrum of individuals from law firms, companies, and institutions involved directly or indirectly in the practice of patent, trademark, copyright, trade secret, and unfair competition law, as well as other fields of law affecting intellectual property. Members represent both owners and users of intellectual property.

Saul Lefkowitz Moot Court Competition

Sponsored by the International Trademark Association (INTA)
Trademark & Unfair Competition

About the Competition

The Saul Lefkowitz Moot Court Competition is an annual event honoring Saul Lefkowitz, whose distinguished career was dedicated to the development of trademark and unfair competition law. The Competition introduces law students to important issues arising in U.S. trademark and unfair competition law while providing an opportunity to develop brief-writing and oral advocacy skills in a mock courtroom setting. The Competition is open to teams of students from accredited U.S. law schools. Approximately 80 teams participate each year.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Who may participate in the Competition?
The Saul Lefkowitz Moot Court Competition is open to teams from accredited U.S. law schools. Eligibility requirements and participation rules for the 2027 Competition will be published with the official competition launch.
How many students may participate on a team?
Team composition requirements are outlined in the official Competition Rules, which are updated annually and will be published when registration opens.
Do teams need a faculty advisor or coach?
Teams are encouraged to work with a faculty advisor, coach, or practitioner mentor. IPLS may help pair interested teams with a competition coach. Additional guidance will be included in the Competition Rules.
How is the Competition structured?
Participating teams submit a written brief and compete in regional oral arguments. Teams advancing from the regional rounds move on to the National Finals Competition.
What topics does the Competition cover?
The Competition focuses on issues arising in U.S. trademark and unfair competition law. A new Competition Problem is drafted each year.
When will registration open?
Information regarding registration, competition timelines, and key deadlines for the 2027 Competition will be posted in Fall 2026.
Where will the Regional Oral Arguments be held?
Regional competition locations and dates will be announced as part of the official 2027 Competition launch.
Is financial assistance available?
Yes. The INTA Foundation offers travel scholarship opportunities to eligible teams participating in the Regional Oral Arguments. Additionally, IPLS offers sponsorship support for organization member teams.
Where can I find past Competition materials?
Reference materials from the 2026 Competition, including the Competition Problem and Competition Rules, are available on the INTA website. Please note that materials are updated annually and should be used for reference purposes only.
Who can I contact with questions?
Questions regarding the Competition may be directed to competitions@inta.org or to the IPLS Co-Presidents.

PTAB Law School Moot Court Competition

Presented by the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) & the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB), in collaboration with Indiana University Maurer School of Law
Patent Trial & PTAB Law

About the Competition

Announced in October 2026 for “the PTAB advocates of tomorrow,” the PTAB Law School Moot Court Competition is a premier national advocacy event organized by the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO), America's Innovation Agency, and the Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB), in collaboration with Indiana University Maurer School of Law (IU Maurer). The competition provides law students across the country with a unique opportunity to develop specialized written and oral advocacy skills in the context of an administrative patent trial proceeding.

Teams participating in the competition will have the rare chance to work directly with active Administrative Patent Judges (APJs) to gain practical tips, courtroom experience, and strategic insight into presenting a case and building an effective record before the PTAB.

About the USPTO & Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB)

The United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) is America’s Innovation Agency, responsible for granting U.S. patents and registering trademarks. The Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB) is an adjudicative body within the USPTO established under the America Invents Act (AIA) that conducts administrative trials, including Inter Partes Review (IPR) and Post-Grant Review (PGR), and decides appeals from adverse patent examiner decisions.
